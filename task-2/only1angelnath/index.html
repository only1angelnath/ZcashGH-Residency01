1. Zcash Origins Timeline

Key milestones that led to Zcash and its privacy technology.

2013

Zerocoin Research Paper

Matthew Green, Ian Miers and Christina Garman (Johns Hopkins) publish the Zerocoin protocol — a proposal to add strong privacy to Bitcoin using zero-knowledge proofs.

2014

Zerocash Research Paper

The Zerocash paper (Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, Garman, Green, Miers, Tromer, Virza) introduces zk-SNARKs that hide sender, receiver and amount, forming the cryptographic foundation of Zcash.

Oct 2016

Launch of Zcash

Zcash mainnet launches (Sprout era) under the leadership of Zooko Wilcox and the Electric Coin Company. The first shielded pool goes live after a multi-party trusted setup ceremony.

Oct 2018

Sapling Upgrade

Sapling activates. Shielded transactions become dramatically faster and more efficient. New address format begins with zs. This is a major usability leap for private payments.

May 2022

Orchard & Unified Addresses (NU5)

Network Upgrade 5 introduces the Orchard shielded pool (using Halo 2 proofs — no new trusted setup) and Unified Addresses that can contain multiple receiver types in one string.

2025–2026

Modern Full-Node Era

zcashd is retired. Zebra (Zcash Foundation) becomes the primary reference full node. Zakura (a high-performance fork of Zebra) appears, focused on speed, pruning and future scalability.

2. Zcash Network Architecture

How information flows from the blockchain to your wallet.

🖥️

Full Node

Zebra or Zakura

  • Validates every block & transaction
  • Holds the complete chain
  • Enforces consensus rules
→
📡

Light Client / Indexer

lightwalletd or Zaino

  • Extracts compact block data
  • Serves only what wallets need
  • Reduces bandwidth & storage
→
📱

Wallet / Application

Zashi, YWallet, etc.

  • Scans for user notes
  • Creates & signs transactions
  • Shows balances privately

How the flow works

  1. Full nodes download and independently verify the entire Zcash blockchain. They are the source of truth.
  2. A light-client service (indexer) connects to one or more full nodes, filters the data into a compact format, and exposes a simple API.
  3. Wallets connect to the light-client service. They download only the compact data needed to detect incoming payments and build new transactions — without running a full node themselves.

This architecture lets ordinary users enjoy strong privacy on mobile phones while still relying on the security of the full network.

3. Full-Node Comparison

Zebra (Zcash Foundation) and Zakura (performance-focused fork). Note: the task mentioned “Sakura” — the actual project name is Zakura.

Aspect Zebra Zakura
Purpose Independent, rigorously reviewed reference full-node implementation maintained by the Zcash Foundation. High-performance consensus-compatible full node (forked from Zebra) focused on speed, pruning and future scale.
Basic Requirements Modern multi-core CPU, several GB of RAM, significant disk space for a full archival node. Can run with lower resources, especially when using pruning or snapshots.
Storage Options Primarily archival (full history). Large disk footprint (hundreds of GB). Native pruning + published snapshots. Can run with ~10–15 GB in pruned mode.
Benefits of Running a Full Node
  • Independently verify every transaction and block (no need to trust third parties)
  • Help decentralise and secure the network
  • Can serve light-client indexers
  • Maximum privacy and sovereignty
